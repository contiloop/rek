# Research procedure [observed: T1–N8]

You receive the main topic, a one-sentence trigger (or "none") and the date. Nothing else about the author is given or needed. [observed: U2]
Read nothing dated after the date; for a wiki, use a version saved before the date, and treat a later version as unconfirmed. [observed: R11]
`instances.md` in this folder lists sites and queries that reached sources before; they are hints you may use or ignore. [observed: R1–R11]
`research-method.md` in this folder holds the full rules with evidence; this file is the order of work. [observed: T1–N8]

## Step 1: Research checklist [observed: T1–U5]

### Trigger [observed: T1–T3]
- What changed, on which day, and how does it touch the home country's money, supply or daily life? [observed: T1]
- If the trigger is "none", what turn of the subject's own season or yearly cycle falls near the date? [observed: T1]
- Read the domestic reports of the trigger from its first hours; keep the one whose quotation is fullest and the one whose numbers are cleanest. [inferred: T2, T3]
- Open the foreign original behind those reports only for what they leave out. [inferred: T3]

### Back chain: how did the subject come to be what it is? [observed: B1–B6]
- Step back one cause at a time from the present to the event where the present form began. [observed: B1]
- Who founded it with whom, what does its name record, and in which crisis or deal did it change hands? [inferred: B2]
- What did the subject or its actors do before the present role, and what ended the old role? [observed: B3]
- Sources: a wiki article, the subject's own history page, a local institution's history page, a research paper. [observed: B4]
- Close the background with one fact about the subject's present size or standing, next to the home country. [observed: B5]
- Stop when one more step back would not change how the reader understands the present form. [observed: B6]

### Base: one skeleton for the background [observed: U1–U5]
- Pick one skeleton document that already tells the back chain in order: a wiki article, a long feature, an expert column, dated before the date. [observed: U1, U2]
- Prior-post slot: where an author would lean on an earlier write-up of their own, find such a different base source instead. [observed: U2]
- Follow the skeleton's order; update only the numbers and facts that changed. [observed: U1]
- Replace a skeleton sentence that a newer source sharpens or contradicts; delete it if nothing replaces it. [observed: U5]
- For every stretch the skeleton does not cover, use one document per stretch. [observed: U3]

### Front chain: what does it mean now? [observed: F1–F10]
- Money: the amount at stake, the price before and after the change, how long the outlay takes to earn back. [observed: F1]
- Size: the subject as a share of the home country's yearly use or of the whole activity it belongs to. [observed: F2]
- Next decision: who holds it, what deadline or date, what would make it change hands. [observed: F3]
- Condition: what limit or condition must the next step meet, who set it, how far is it met? [inferred: F4]
- Behind the actor: equity partner, owner of record of the main asset, the person who controls that owner. [inferred: F5]
- Path: the physical route from source to home-country user, any third place on it, and the cost, fee or risk that makes it the route used. [observed: F6]
- Problem: for each gain, the problem that limits it. [observed: F7]
- Motive: why does the actor act now, and what is its own stake? [inferred: F8]
- Home-country side: which home-country companies, people, government or counterpart are involved, and what happens to them? [observed: F9]
- Stop when you have the money figure, the home-country fact, and the next decision with its date. [observed: F10]

### Where the rare sources are [observed: R1–R11]
- Follow every source a domestic article names (foreign outlet, data provider, original statement) and open it. [observed: R1]
- Translate the subject's numbers and proper nouns into English or the place's local language, and search with them. [observed: R2]
- Run long descriptive queries in the post's language that chain slot names with descriptive words: "[subject] [figure name] [year] [what changed]". [inferred: R3]
- Trade papers: "[subject in English] [event word] [month year]"; if paywalled, read the free abstract and an article that cites it. [observed: R4]
- Small domestic outlets: "[subject in the post's language] [number or event word]" on a news portal; if the original is closed, use a reprint. [observed: R5]
- Official figures: "[issuing body] [figure name] [year]" in statistics tables, government releases, fact sheets; if deleted, an archived copy. [inferred: R6]
- Database record of the main asset: owner, operator, manager, earlier names. [inferred: R7]
- Original statement or social post of the actor; quote it from the domestic translation that matches it; if behind a login, an article showing a capture. [inferred: R8]
- Far background abroad: English research papers with "[place] [activity] history". [inferred: R9]
- Local media of the place in its own language. [observed: R10]
- Niche details: a wiki article in the post's language, version before the date. [observed: R11]
- Do not stop at the first page of an ordinary search on the topic. [observed: N8]

## Step 2: Build the development units (research.md) [observed: B1, U1, U4]

- Connect what you found as back chain → present state → front chain, one link per unit. [observed: B1]
- Put the trigger and the newest material in the last units, after the skeleton's chain. [observed: U4]
- Attach to each unit the sources that carry it; every number, date, name and quotation has one. [inferred: A9]
- Write down the additions you will make, each built only from sourced facts: [inferred: A9]
  - one ratio or multiple tying the subject to a total the reader knows; [inferred: A1]
  - the arithmetic that shows the gap to the next threshold, where there is one; [inferred: A2]
  - the headline number set against a reference number, and what that implies; [observed: A6]
  - one sentence joining two sourced facts into a reading no single source states; [observed: A4]
  - your guesses, marked as guesses. [observed: A5]

## Step 3: Put it in the frame (optional) [observed: W1–W14]

- Follow `writing-structure.md` in this folder; it is optional, and the research above comes first. [observed: W1–W14]
- Gloss each foreign abbreviation in parentheses at first use. [observed: A3]
- Explain only the one term the outcome depends on, with an analogy from a domestic field the reader knows. [inferred: A10]
- Put a short turn sentence where the reader would be surprised or where the next actor reacts. [observed: A7]
- Close with the one-line comment: a takeaway or the next point to watch, ending on a fact or a comparison. [observed: A8]

## Step 4: Self-check [observed: N1–N8]

- No source links, no source list, no footnotes; name an outlet only when the fact is its own finding. [observed: N1]
- No summary or contents opening; at most one framing line before the first fact. [observed: N2]
- No direct quotation in a foreign language; use the domestic translation. [observed: N3]
- No headings, bullet points or tables in the post body. [observed: N4]
- No account of the research, the tools, or disagreements between sources. [observed: N5]
- No pros-and-cons section and no closing generality. [observed: N6]
- No primer on the general field. [observed: N7]
- No fact in the post that is not in research.md. [inferred: A9]
