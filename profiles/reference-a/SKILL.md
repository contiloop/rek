# Research procedure [observed: T1–N8]

You receive the main topic, a one-sentence trigger (or "none") and the date; nothing else about the author is given or needed. [observed: U1]
Read nothing dated after the date; for a wiki, use the version in force before the date, and treat a later version as unconfirmed. [observed: R11]
`instances.md` in this folder lists sites and queries that reached sources before; they are hints you may use or ignore. [observed: R1–R13]
`research-method.md` in this folder holds the full rules with evidence; this file is the order of work. [observed: T1–N8]

## Step 1: Research checklist [observed: T1–U5]

### Trigger [observed: T1–T4]
- Where does the subject meet the home country's money, supply, industry or daily life? [observed: T1]
- If there is a trigger: what changed, on which day, and who reported it first in the post's language? [inferred: T2]
- Read the domestic reports of the trigger from its first hours; keep the one whose quotation is fullest and the one whose numbers are cleanest. [inferred: T2, T3]
- Open the foreign original behind those reports only for what they leave out. [inferred: T3]
- If the trigger is "none": which turn of the subject's season, or which recent report on its present standing, falls near the date? [inferred: T4]
- With no trigger, the post spends most of its length on the back chain and ends on the present. [inferred: T4]

### Back chain: how did the subject come to be what it is? [observed: B1–B7]
- Step back one cause at a time from the present form to where that form began. [observed: B1]
- Who founded it with whom, what does its name record, and in which crisis, rule or deal did it change hands? [inferred: B2]
- What did the subject or its actors do before the present role, and what ended the old role? [observed: B3]
- Which outside shock or sudden demand, perhaps from an unrelated field, pushed it into its present role, and how did that reach it? [observed: B6]
- Sources: a wiki article, the subject's own history page, a local institution's history page, a research paper, a long feature series. [observed: B4, R13]
- Before leaving the background, give one fact about the subject's size or standing at that point, next to the home country. [observed: B5]
- Stop rule: keep asking "what came before that, and what did it start from?" [observed: B7]
- Go on until the answer is the point where the subject's story can begin: what existed before it took any form, and the first step that set it going. [observed: B7]
- Do not stop early because a nearer step already explains the present. [observed: B7]
- If the skeleton you chose starts later than that point, extend the chain backward before its first item. [observed: B7, U3]

### Base: one skeleton for the background [observed: U1–U5]
- Pick one skeleton document dated before the date that already tells the back chain in order: a wiki article, a long feature, an expert column, a history page. [observed: U1, B4]
- Follow the skeleton's order; update only the numbers and facts that changed. [observed: U1]
- Drop the skeleton's passages that served its own occasion: its news peg, its forecasts, its closing. [observed: U5]
- Replace a skeleton sentence that a newer source sharpens or contradicts (estimate by data, old rank by present rank); soften it or delete it if nothing replaces it. [observed: U4]
- For every stretch the skeleton does not cover, use one document per stretch. [observed: U2]
- Put new material in time order: earlier history before the skeleton's first item, the newest development after its last. [observed: U3]

### Front chain: what does it mean now? [observed: F1–F11]
- Money: the amount at stake, the price or fee before and after the change, how long the outlay takes to earn back. [observed: F1]
- Size: the subject as a share of the home country's yearly use, or its rank among all others of its kind. [observed: F2]
- Next decision: who holds it, what deadline or date, what would make it change hands. [inferred: F3]
- Condition: what limit or condition must the next step meet, who set it, how far is it met? [inferred: F4]
- Behind the actor: equity partner, owner of record of the main asset, the person who controls that owner. [inferred: F5]
- Path: the physical route from source to home-country user, any third place on it, and the cost, fee or risk that makes it the route used. [observed: F6]
- Problem: for each gain, the problem that limits it. [observed: F7]
- Motive: why does the actor act now, and what is its own stake? [inferred: F8]
- Home-country side: which home-country companies, people, government or counterpart are involved, what happens to them, and how did they answer? [observed: F9]
- Rival: the subject's nearest rival or alternative, compared on the point that decides the choice. [observed: F10]
- Stop rule: keep asking "and what follows from that, for whom?" [observed: F11]
- Go on until the answer reaches past the date: where each party and place the chain brought in stands on the date. [observed: F11]
- Include what is still open after the date, with its due time when one exists. [observed: F11, F3]
- Include what that would change for the home-country reader. [observed: F11, F9]

### Where the rare sources are [observed: R1–R13]
- Follow every source a domestic article names (foreign outlet, data provider, original statement) and open it. [inferred: R1]
- Translate the subject's numbers and proper nouns into English or the place's local language, and search with them. [observed: R2]
- Run long descriptive queries in the post's language that chain slot names with figures: "[subject] [figure] [year] [what changed]". [inferred: R3]
- Trade papers: "[subject in English] [event word] [month year]"; if paywalled, read the free abstract and an article that cites it. [inferred: R4]
- Small domestic outlets: "[subject in the post's language] [number or event word]" on a news portal; if the original is closed, use a reprint. [observed: R5]
- Official figures: "[issuing body] [figure name] [year]" in statistics tables, government releases, fact sheets; if deleted, an archived copy. [inferred: R6]
- Database record of the main asset: owner, operator, manager, earlier names. [inferred: R7]
- Original statement or social post of the actor; quote it from the domestic translation that matches it; if behind a login, an article showing a capture. [inferred: R8]
- Far background abroad: English research papers and research-institution articles with "[place or person] [activity] history". [inferred: R9]
- Local media of the place in its own language. [inferred: R10]
- Niche details: a wiki article, version in force before the date. [observed: R11]
- Community boards, personal blogs, user-made wikis, video descriptions: pointers to retellings and old tables; keep a figure only from a dated page. [inferred: R12]
- Domestic feature series and columns on the subject's history: "[subject in the post's language] [first year or founding figure] [history word]". [observed: R13]
- Do not stop at the first page of an ordinary search on the topic. [observed: N8]

## Step 2: Build the development units (research.md) [observed: B1, B7, F11, U3]

- Connect what you found as back chain → main topic → front chain, one link per unit. [observed: B1]
- The first unit is where the back-chain stop rule ended; the last units are where the front-chain stop rule ended. [observed: B7, F11]
- Put the trigger and the newest material in the last units, after the skeleton's chain. [observed: U3]
- Attach to each unit the sources that carry it; every number, date, name and quotation has one. [inferred: A9]
- Write down the additions you will make, each built only from sourced facts: [inferred: A9]
  - one ratio, multiple or count over a list tying the subject to a total the reader knows; [inferred: A1]
  - the arithmetic that shows the gap to the next threshold, where there is one; [inferred: A2]
  - the headline number set against a reference number, and what that implies; [observed: A6]
  - one sentence joining two sourced facts into a reading no single source states; [observed: A4]
  - your guesses and any hearsay, marked as such. [observed: A5]

## Step 3: Put it in the frame (optional) [observed: W1–W14]

- Follow `writing-structure.md` in this folder; it is optional, and the research above comes first. [observed: W1–W14]
- Gloss each foreign abbreviation, technical term or renamed place in parentheses at first use. [observed: A3]
- Explain only the one term or role the outcome depends on, with an analogy from a domestic field or service the reader knows. [inferred: A10]
- Put a short turn sentence where the reader would be surprised or where the next actor reacts. [observed: A7]
- Close with the one-line comment: a takeaway, a judgement about the home country or the next point to watch, ending on a fact or a comparison. [observed: A8]

## Step 4: Self-check [observed: N1–N8]

- The first unit reaches as far back as the back-chain stop rule, and the last reaches past the date. [observed: B7, F11]
- No source links, no source list, no footnotes; name an outlet only when the fact is its own finding. [observed: N1]
- No summary or contents opening; at most a brief framing remark before the first fact. [observed: N2]
- No direct quotation in a foreign language; use the domestic translation. [observed: N3]
- No headings, bullet points or tables in the post body. [observed: N4]
- No account of the research, the tools, or disagreements between sources. [observed: N5]
- No pros-and-cons section and no closing generality. [observed: N6]
- No primer on the general field. [observed: N7]
- No fact that is not in research.md, in the body, in image captions or in image descriptions. [inferred: A9]
