# 0008 Extract rules as the author's moves, never as instances

**Situation.** The first profile (64 rules) had memorized the three material posts. Query templates carried their nouns (fishery, Mtpa, Senate, 到着), stop conditions were ratios counted from three posts (backward 18–30%, forward 44–47%), and the checklist assumed "the author wrote about this topic before". The round-2 scores with the material's traces removed (units found 9→4, 10→7, 8→3) showed the profile was recall, not procedure. The cause was the brief: a 40-word quotation per rule and `[observed]` at 2 of 3 posts pushed the worker to instance-level rules.

**Decision.** The method-extraction brief writes, for every source and action, three lines (instance, move, reason) and makes rules only from the move and the reason; asks "why did the author do this" once more and adopts the answer when it fits more topics; merges two instances of one move into their common ancestor; forbids proper and domain nouns in rule sentences (heading lines and every line of `SKILL.md`; nouns live only on `- evidence:` / `- why:` / `- try:` lines); instantiates every rule on two hypothetical topics from unrelated fields and rewrites one level up what cannot be instantiated; marks `[observed]` only when every post supports the rule. Output is two tiers: rules (`research-method.md`, `SKILL.md`, `writing-structure.md` as moves without measured values) and hints (`instances.md`: sites, queries, measured numbers, the move table), which the reproduction and research workers may read as hints. The noun check is the worker's self-check; after a run the orchestrator greps for the material's nouns.

**Rejected options.** A code check for nouns: no deterministic definition of "domain noun" exists, and a term list built from the runs would miss what matters; the user chose self-check. A cap on the rule count: the count is a result, not a rule. Widening the material to posts from other domains now: the real fix, but it costs a full trace per post; deferred until the test results say whether the brief change alone is enough.

**Result.** Re-extraction from the same three runs gave 53 rules (T3 B6 F10 R11 U5 A10 N8), all passing the noun check, six rewritten one level up; `SKILL.md` went from 188 to 91 lines. The worker replaced the second default test topic with a university-admission revision because one material post is about a food product. The hold-out variants were deleted: a new post is tested before it becomes material, so no variant is needed.
